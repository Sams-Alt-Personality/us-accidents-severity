PROBLEM / GAP BEING ADDRESSED

Base paper: Tang et al. (2025), "Research on Traffic Accident Severity Level
Prediction Model Based on Improved Machine Learning", Systems, 13(1), 31.
It predicts accident severity on the Kaggle US Accidents dataset using
Decision Tree, XGBoost and Random Forest, with resampling for class imbalance.
Its feature set is built from weather, road and location variables, and it
does not report engineering time-based features.

Gap: Time of day, day of week and season are well-established risk factors in
the traffic safety literature, but they are not explicitly engineered or
evaluated in the base paper. The base paper also reports only overall accuracy,
recall and F1, which are dominated by the majority class (about 92% of
accidents are Severity 2). Performance on the rare, more serious classes
(Severity 1, 3 and 4) is not examined.

Our contribution: We add temporal features (hour, day of week, month, season,
weekend flag, rush-hour flag) to the same model family on the same dataset and
test their effect in a controlled ablation, with and without SMOTE
oversampling, using macro-averaged metrics and per-class recall.

Result (Random Forest): Adding temporal features raised macro F1 from 0.553 to
0.588 and Severity-1 recall from 0.527 to 0.705. Combined with SMOTE, macro F1
reached 0.594, Severity-1 recall 0.826, and Severity-4 recall rose from 0.054
to 0.156. Severity 4 remains the hardest class to detect.
